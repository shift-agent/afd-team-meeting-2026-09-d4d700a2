Trust & assumptions · 10 minutes

Ask first.

Have you ever been sure you knew why someone did something and later found out you were wrong?

Quick show of hands. No stories required.

Trust breaks when we fill a gap with an assumption instead of a question.

Trust & assumptions

Three places assumptions hide

In our thoughts
“She is late again, she does not care.” We decide the reason before we know it.
In our words
“Everyone knows the new patient flow.” We skip saying it because it feels obvious.
In our actions
We reschedule, skip a step, or cover for someone without asking, and it lands wrong.

About habits, not people. Optional: a 60-second story of assuming the worst about someone, then learning to ask.

Trust & assumptions

The ASK practice

  1. Is it true? Do I have all the facts?
  2. Is it necessary? Is it my responsibility to communicate?
  3. Is it helpful? If I communicate, will I be able to say it the right way, without assumption?
  4. Is it timely? Am I communicating when it will be well received?
Pairs, 90 seconds: one describes a small, real work moment, the other only asks and checks.

Not only for what we say out loud. It is how we catch an assumption before it acts.

Trust & assumptions

Our team agreement, next 30 days

  1. Ask before assuming.
  2. Say the obvious out loud.
  3. Check back before acting.

The monthly check-in is where we say what is true, not what we think others want to hear.

Discuss: Think of a time this month you assumed you knew why someone did something. What happened when you checked? What is one assumption you will drop this week?
